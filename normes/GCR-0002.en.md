## Summary

This standard states how an African certification authority is created, keeps its keys and issues certificates. The certificates are ordinary X.509 certificates: they serve websites, mail, document and code signing, and identity, like all others, and are read by all existing software.

What sets such an authority apart comes down to three points. All its keys come from the generator of GCR-0001 and were replayed before being used. Its root is born during a ceremony written in advance, before witnesses, on a machine with no network. Its certificate chain follows a profile that twelve checks, runnable by a program, are enough to verify.

## 1. Purpose and scope

1.1. This standard applies to any certification authority that declares it issues conforming certificates, and to the certificates it issues.

1.2. It changes neither the X.509 format, nor the signature algorithms, nor the protocols that use certificates.

1.3. It does not define the verification of the identity of natural persons beyond the requirements of section 8; that verification falls under the law of each country.

1.4. It does not address the listing of a root in the trust stores of systems and browsers. Section 12 states what follows from that.

## 2. Conventions

2.1. The words **MUST**, **MUST NOT**, **SHOULD**, **SHOULD NOT** and **MAY**, in capitals, have the meaning defined by RFC 2119 and RFC 8174. They translate the French words DOIT, NE DOIT PAS, DEVRAIT, NE DEVRAIT PAS and PEUT.

2.2. A requirement that can be verified on the certificates alone is verified by a K check of section 11. A requirement that bears on the conduct of the authority is verified by a documentary P check of the same section.

2.3. The names of certificate fields and extensions are those of RFC 5280.

## 3. Definitions

3.1. **Root**: authority whose certificate is signed by its own key, and from which all trust follows.

3.2. **Issuing authority**: authority whose certificate is signed by the root, and which signs end-entity certificates.

3.3. **End-entity certificate**: certificate handed to a subscriber, which cannot sign any other certificate.

3.4. **Subscriber**: person or organisation to whom an end-entity certificate is issued.

3.5. **Ceremony**: session during which an authority key is generated, replayed and placed in custody.

3.6. **Record**: the generation record defined by GCR-0001, section 8.

3.7. **Register**: public list, kept by the authority, of all the certificates it has issued.

## 4. Architecture

4.1. A conforming authority MUST have at least two tiers: a root, and one or more issuing authorities.

4.2. The root MUST sign only issuing authority certificates and revocation lists. It MUST NOT sign any end-entity certificate.

4.3. An issuing authority MUST NOT be able to create a sub-authority.

4.4. The root key MUST stay offline: the machine that carries it is connected to no network, and serves only for ceremonies.

4.5. The key of an issuing authority MAY be online. It SHOULD be kept in hardware from which it cannot be extracted.

## 5. Keys

5.1. Every authority key MUST be generated according to GCR-0001.

5.2. The root key MUST be 4096 bits long. The key of an issuing authority MUST be at least 3072 bits long.

5.3. The root key and that of each issuing authority MUST reach level 2 of GCR-0001 before any signature: replayed by an independent implementation, on another machine.

5.4. The record of each authority key MUST be published with the corresponding certificate.

5.5. The key of an end-entity certificate MUST be an RSA key of at least 2048 bits, with public exponent 65537. It SHOULD be generated according to GCR-0001.

5.6. The key of an end-entity certificate MUST be generated by the subscriber or under their sole custody. The authority MUST NOT generate, receive or keep the private key of a subscriber.

5.7. The seed of an authority key follows GCR-0001, requirement 5.6. If it is kept, it MUST be kept apart from the private key, in the custody of different people.

## 6. Ceremony

6.1. Every authority key MUST be born during a ceremony.

6.2. The script of the ceremony MUST be written before the session: every gesture, every command, in order. The session follows the script; any deviation is recorded in it.

6.3. The ceremony MUST bring together at least three people, one of whom does not belong to the organisation that operates the authority. None of them is left alone with the machine.

6.4. Generation MUST take place on a machine with no network, whose system and programs were verified by their fingerprints before the session.

6.5. The level 2 replay MUST take place during the session, on a second machine with no network, with an independent implementation. The key is retained only if both machines give the same modulus.

6.6. The private key MUST be backed up in at least two separate places, in such a way that no single person can reconstitute it.

6.7. The ceremony MUST give rise to minutes, signed by everyone present, which carry: the date and place, the names and roles, the fingerprints of the programs used, the record of the key, the fingerprint of the certificate, the fate of the seed and the deviations from the script.

6.8. The minutes MUST be published. They MUST NOT contain a seed or a private key. They SHOULD be timestamped by a means anyone can verify.

## 7. Certificate profile

7.1. Every certificate MUST be an X.509 version 3 certificate, signed with RSA and SHA-256, SHA-384 or SHA-512.

7.2. Every serial number MUST be positive, unique for the authority that issues it, 8 to 20 bytes long, and carry at least 64 bits of randomness.

7.3. Every certificate MUST carry the identifier of its key (`subjectKeyIdentifier`). Every certificate other than the root MUST carry the identifier of its issuer's key (`authorityKeyIdentifier`), equal to the key identifier written in the issuer's certificate.

7.4. The name of every authority MUST include the country (`C`), the organisation (`O`) and a common name (`CN`).

7.5. The validity period of a certificate MUST be contained within that of its issuer's certificate.

7.6. The following table fixes the rest of the profile:

| | Root | Issuing authority | End-entity certificate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Signed by | its own key | the root | an issuing authority |
| `basicConstraints` (critical) | `CA:TRUE` | `CA:TRUE`, `pathLenConstraint` = 0 | `CA:FALSE` |
| `keyUsage` (critical) | `keyCertSign`, `cRLSign`, nothing else | `keyCertSign`, `cRLSign`, nothing else | according to use, never `keyCertSign` |
| `extendedKeyUsage` | absent | MAY | MUST be present |
| `subjectAltName` | absent | absent | MUST be present |
| `cRLDistributionPoints` | absent | MUST be present | MUST be present |
| Maximum lifetime | 15 years | 6 years | 2 years |

7.7. The end-entity certificate of a server SHOULD last 398 days at most.

## 8. Issuance

8.1. Before issuing, the authority MUST verify that the applicant is indeed the one the certificate will name: control of the domain name for a server, of the address for mail, identity established on documents for a person or an organisation.

8.2. The request MUST be signed by the applicant's private key, which proves they hold it.

8.3. The authority MUST refuse a key that is not an RSA key of at least 2048 bits with exponent 65537.

8.4. The authority MUST refuse a key whose modulus already appears in its register under the name of another subscriber.

8.5. The authority SHOULD refuse a key whose modulus shares a factor with a modulus of its register.

8.6. If the applicant attaches a record, the authority MUST verify that the modulus and the fingerprint of the record are those of the key of the request. The authority never receives the seed: the level reached is declared by the subscriber.

8.7. The authority MUST NOT backdate a certificate.

## 9. Revocation and register

9.1. Each authority MUST publish a revocation list (RFC 5280) at the address written in the certificates it issues.

9.2. The list of an issuing authority MUST be renewed at least every seven days, and within twenty-four hours of a revocation. That of the root MUST be renewed at least every twelve months, and at each revocation.

9.3. A certificate MUST be revoked as soon as its private key is compromised or suspected to be, the subscriber asks for it, or a statement in the certificate proves false.

9.4. An authority MAY additionally offer an online certificate status service.

9.5. Each authority MUST keep a public register of all the certificates it has issued: serial number, name of the subscriber, dates, SHA-256 fingerprint of the certificate. A line once entered is neither altered nor removed.

9.6. A certificate absent from the register MUST be held as not issued by the authority, and revoked if it bears its signature.

9.7. The compromise of an issuing authority key entails the revocation of its certificate by the root, during a ceremony. The compromise of the root key ends the authority: it MUST be announced publicly without delay.

## 10. End of life

10.1. An issuing authority stops issuing when the remaining lifetime of its certificate is shorter than the lifetime of the longest certificate it issues.

10.2. At the end of an authority, its private key and, where applicable, its seed MUST be destroyed during a ceremony, and the minutes published.

10.3. The register, the revocation lists and the minutes MUST remain public for at least ten years after the end of the authority.

## 11. Conformity checks

### 11.1 Checks on the certificates

11.1.1. A verifier receives three certificates (root, issuing authority, end-entity certificate) and the records of the root and of the issuing authority. It MUST run the following twelve checks and return the list of those that fail.

| No. | Check |
|---|---|
| K1 | The three certificates are X.509 version 3 certificates. |
| K2 | The root is signed by its own key, and its issuer is its subject. |
| K3 | The root is an authority (`CA:TRUE`, critical) whose usage, critical, is limited to signing certificates and lists; its name carries the country, the organisation and a common name. |
| K4 | The issuing authority is signed by the root; it is an authority with no sub-authority (`pathLenConstraint` = 0, critical), with the same limited usage and an equally complete name. |
| K5 | The end-entity certificate is signed by the issuing authority and not by the root; it is not an authority (`CA:FALSE`, critical); its usage is critical and without `keyCertSign`; it carries `subjectAltName` and `extendedKeyUsage`. |
| K6 | The three keys are RSA keys with exponent 65537: 4096 bits for the root, at least 3072 for the issuing authority, at least 2048 for the end-entity certificate. |
| K7 | The three signatures use SHA-256, SHA-384 or SHA-512. |
| K8 | The lifetimes respect the ceilings of 7.6, and each is contained within that of the issuer. |
| K9 | The serial numbers are positive and 8 to 20 bytes long. |
| K10 | The issuing authority and the end-entity certificate carry a revocation list address. |
| K11 | The key identifiers are present, and the issuer identifier written in each certificate is that of the issuer's certificate. |
| K12 | The root and the issuing authority each have a record whose modulus and fingerprint are those of the certificate. |

11.1.2. The verifier returns CONFORMING if the twelve checks pass. It reads neither private key nor seed.

### 11.2 Documentary checks

11.2.1. The following requirements cannot be read in the certificates. An auditor verifies them on the documents published by the authority.

| No. | Document | Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Ceremony minutes for each authority key | 6.1 to 6.8 |
| P2 | Declaration of level 2 for each authority key | 5.3 |
| P3 | Revocation lists, up to date at the addresses written | 9.1, 9.2 |
| P4 | Public register of issued certificates | 9.5 |

## 12. Security, limits and versions

12.1. **What the standard guarantees.** The keys on which the whole chain depends were made by a public computation and replayed by a second program before witnesses. The shape of the chain is verified by program, without asking anything of the authority.

12.2. **What it does not guarantee.** It does not guarantee the daily conduct of the authority: the rigour of identity verification, the custody of keys, the keeping of the register. These points fall under audit and the law of the country.

12.3. **Read by all, trusted by those who list it.** A conforming certificate is read by all software. For it to be trusted without any setting, the root must appear in the trust store of the system or browser. The publishers of those stores set their own conditions, including yearly audits. A root MAY serve before that listing wherever those who rely on it install it themselves: an administration, a company, a network of institutions, or a whole country if the law recognises it.

12.4. **Lifetime of RSA.** The ceilings of 7.6 hold as long as RSA serves. GCR-0001, requirement 11.6, applies here: a root created today for fifteen years SHOULD provide, in its script, for the way to give it a successor.

12.5. **Versions.** A certificate remains conforming to the version under which it was issued. Comments on this preliminary draft are received by the maintainer, and each acknowledged defect is recorded with its correction.

## Annex A — Status of the reference implementation

A.1. `autorite/autorite.py` creates a 4096-bit root, a 3072-bit issuing authority and end-entity certificates, all keys coming from the generator of GCR-0001, certificates written by OpenSSL according to the profile of section 7.

A.2. `autorite/controle.py` runs the twelve K checks. A chain produced by the tool passes them all, and OpenSSL verifies it in strict mode. Ten faulty chains, built on purpose, are each stopped by the expected check: end-entity certificate signed by the root, without a name, turned into an authority, too long, without a revocation address; issuing authority with too short a key, or able to create sub-authorities; authority without country or organisation in its name; record of another key; missing record.

A.3. The tool does not yet produce the revocation lists or the register of sections 9.1 and 9.5, and today issues server certificates. These points remain to be written.
